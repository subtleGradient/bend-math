# Success criteria

## Scope and binary checks

- Every selected claim has a source locator or is explicitly labeled a fixture.
- Every generated declaration maps to a claim or supporting definition.
- Every unresolved meaning has an ID and affected declarations.
- Every numeric approximation is labeled as different from exact semantics.
- Compilation, semantic review, proof, and empirical support have separate status.
- Rendered equations have copyable source.
- No story is required to complete the transcription.
- No unproved statement becomes an axiom just to clear a checker.
- No existing human-owned law is silently edited.
- Bend 2 output is primary; Lean references address identified gaps, not replace it.
- Supported packages are preferred; absent coverage triggers a useful explicit
  generic interface/scoped component, not an automatic blanket no-Bend response.
- Gap entries include operation/law, versioned search evidence, workaround or
  dependency, minimal upstream API proposal, and testable acceptance criterion.
- Catalogued, inspected, and checked packages remain distinct; arithmetic coverage
  is not proof coverage or version compatibility.
- No TypeScript appears without an explicit request.
- Asymmetrical ordered tensor slots retain source meaning; no unprovided symmetry,
  positive definiteness, nondegeneracy, inverse, or domain coercion is assumed.

## Budget

One equation plus its needed context per initial pass. Do not spend the budget on
proof search, a simulator, a theorem-prover setup, or whole-paper extraction.

## Adversarial reasoning

Prior: supported Bend packages plus Lean reference concepts reduce invented notation.
Falsifier: a typechecked declaration may still have the wrong quantifier scope.
Response: require source correspondence review independently of compilation.

Prior: matching Lean and Bend names permit translation.
Falsifier: Bend F32 and Lean Real have different arithmetic.
Response: preserve domain distinctions and useful generic Bend components with
explicit dependencies; block only unsupported portions; no equivalence claim.

Practical risk: a long glossary hides the author's immediate blocker.
Response: hand off the first unresolved question plus a source-linked ledger.
